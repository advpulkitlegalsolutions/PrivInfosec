India’s **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** (DPDP Act) governs the processing of digital personal data in India, and of personal data processed outside India where it relates to offering goods or services to people in India. The **DPDP Rules, 2025** set out how the Act operates and phase obligations in over a transition period.

This checklist summarises the core obligations for a *Data Fiduciary* — the organisation that decides the purpose and means of processing. Timelines and details should always be confirmed against the current notified text.

## 1. Know your data

- Map the personal data you collect, where it comes from, where it is stored and who it is shared with.
- Record the purpose and the basis for each processing activity: consent, or one of the Act’s specified *legitimate uses*.
- Identify any processing of children’s data or data of persons with disabilities who have a lawful guardian.

## 2. Notice and consent

- Provide a clear notice describing the personal data and the purpose of processing, how Data Principals can exercise their rights, and how to complain to the Data Protection Board.
- Make sure consent is free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by clear affirmative action, and limited to the data necessary for the purpose.
- Make withdrawing consent as easy as giving it, and stop processing (and have processors stop) within a reasonable time after withdrawal.

## 3. Data Principal rights

- Set up processes to respond to requests for information about processing, correction, completion, updating and erasure.
- Provide an accessible grievance redressal mechanism and respond within the prescribed period.
- Support the right of Data Principals to nominate another person to exercise their rights.

## 4. Security and breaches

- Implement reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breaches, including appropriate technical and organisational measures for processors.
- Prepare a breach response procedure: intimation to the Data Protection Board and to each affected Data Principal, in the form and within the timelines the Rules prescribe.
- Test the procedure — and keep evidence that you have.

## 5. Retention and erasure

- Erase personal data when the Data Principal withdraws consent or the specified purpose is no longer being served, unless retention is required by law.
- Define retention periods per data category and apply them to processors too.

## 6. Vendors and transfers

- Engage processors only under a valid contract and remain accountable for their processing.
- Check cross-border transfers against any restrictions notified by the Central Government, and against sector-specific rules that may impose stricter requirements.

## 7. Governance

- Publish business contact information of a person who can answer questions about your processing (or of your DPO, where applicable).
- Determine whether you may be notified as a *Significant Data Fiduciary*, which brings additional obligations such as an India-based DPO, an independent data auditor and periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments.
- Train staff who handle personal data and assign clear ownership of each obligation.

<Callout title="A note on timing">
Several DPDP obligations take effect after transition periods set out in the DPDP Rules. Use the time to build processes and evidence — retrofitting under deadline pressure is more expensive.
</Callout>
