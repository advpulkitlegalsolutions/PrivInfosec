Enterprise customers increasingly ask suppliers for independent assurance over information security. The two most common requests are **ISO/IEC 27001** certification and a **SOC 2** report. They overlap substantially, but they are not the same thing.

## At a glance

| | ISO/IEC 27001 | SOC 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | International standard for an Information Security Management System (ISMS) | Attestation framework based on the AICPA Trust Services Criteria |
| Outcome | Certificate issued by an accredited certification body | Attestation report issued by a licensed CPA firm |
| Scope focus | The management system: risk assessment, controls, continual improvement | Controls relevant to security and, optionally, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy |
| Common demand | International, including India, Europe and Asia | Frequently requested by North American customers |
| Cycle | Three-year certification cycle with periodic surveillance audits | Typically renewed annually |

## ISO/IEC 27001 in brief

ISO/IEC 27001 requires an organisation to establish, implement, maintain and continually improve an ISMS. At its core is a risk assessment that drives the selection of controls. The 2022 edition’s Annex A sets out 93 reference controls grouped into organisational, people, physical and technological themes, and organisations document which apply in a *Statement of Applicability*.

## SOC 2 in brief

A SOC 2 examination reports on controls at a service organisation relevant to the Trust Services Criteria. **Security** is always in scope; availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy are optional. A **Type 1** report covers the design of controls at a point in time; a **Type 2** report also tests whether they operated effectively over a period, commonly between three and twelve months.

## Which one first?

Start with your customers. If your pipeline is concentrated in North America, SOC 2 is often the faster commercial win. If you sell internationally or want a management-system foundation, ISO 27001 tends to be the better anchor.

Many organisations do not have to choose permanently. A well-designed control set — risk assessment, access control, asset management, logging, vendor management, incident response, change management — can be mapped to both frameworks, with the evidence collected once and reused.

## What readiness involves

1. Define scope: systems, locations, teams and services.
2. Perform a gap assessment against the chosen framework.
3. Design and implement missing controls, policies and processes.
4. Operate the controls and collect evidence.
5. Conduct an internal audit or readiness review before the external audit.
