A **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** is a structured process for identifying and reducing the privacy risks of a processing activity before it starts. Done well, it is one of the most practical Privacy by Design tools an organisation has.

## When is a DPIA needed?

Under the **EU GDPR** (Article 35), a DPIA is required where processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals’ rights and freedoms. The GDPR gives examples, including:

- systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects, such as profiling, that produces legal or similarly significant effects;
- large-scale processing of special categories of data or data relating to criminal convictions;
- systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale.

Supervisory authorities publish further lists of processing that requires a DPIA.

Under India’s **DPDP Act**, organisations notified as *Significant Data Fiduciaries* must carry out periodic DPIAs. For other organisations, a DPIA remains a sensible way to demonstrate due diligence for higher-risk processing.

## What a DPIA should contain

1. **A description** of the processing: data, purposes, systems, recipients and retention.
2. **Necessity and proportionality**: why the processing is needed and whether less intrusive options exist.
3. **Risk assessment**: the likelihood and severity of harm to individuals.
4. **Measures**: the technical and organisational safeguards that address each risk, with owners.
5. **Sign-off and review**: who accepted the residual risk, and when the assessment will be revisited.

Under the GDPR, if high residual risk remains after mitigation, the supervisory authority must be consulted before processing begins.

## Making DPIAs useful

- **Start early.** A DPIA at design stage can change the design; at launch it can only document it.
- **Use a screening step.** A short questionnaire helps teams decide quickly whether a full DPIA is needed.
- **Involve the right people.** Product, engineering, security and legal each see different risks.
- **Track the actions.** A DPIA whose measures are never implemented gives false comfort.

<Callout title="Proportionate by design">
Not every change needs a full assessment. A clear screening process lets you focus effort on the processing that genuinely carries higher risk.
</Callout>
