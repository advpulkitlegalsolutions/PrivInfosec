A **Virtual DPO** (sometimes called a Fractional DPO or DPO-as-a-Service) is an experienced privacy professional who provides data protection officer-level support on a part-time, retained basis. Instead of hiring a full-time specialist, an organisation draws on senior privacy expertise for a defined number of hours or a defined scope each month.

## Why organisations choose a virtual model

Most organisations need privacy leadership long before they need a full-time privacy department. A virtual arrangement typically suits organisations that:

- process meaningful volumes of personal data but do not yet justify a full-time privacy hire;
- face enterprise customer, investor or partner due diligence on privacy;
- are preparing for India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act or already operate under the EU GDPR;
- need interim cover while recruiting or after a privacy lead has left.

## What the role usually covers

The exact scope is agreed at the start, but a Virtual DPO engagement commonly includes:

1. **Programme oversight** — maintaining a prioritised privacy roadmap and tracking progress against it.
2. **Advisory access** — a point of contact for product, legal, HR, procurement and technology questions.
3. **Documentation** — keeping records of processing, notices and policies current as the business changes.
4. **Assessments** — reviewing or conducting Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) for new or higher-risk processing.
5. **Vendor support** — reviewing processor terms and assessing third parties that handle personal data.
6. **Requests and incidents** — supporting the handling of data subject requests and personal data breaches.
7. **Management reporting** — giving leadership a clear, periodic view of privacy risk and progress.

## Formal designation: check the applicable law

Whether an external provider can be formally designated as your DPO depends on the law that applies to you. Under the GDPR, a DPO may be a staff member or fulfil the role under a service contract. Under the DPDP Act, a *Significant Data Fiduciary* must appoint a Data Protection Officer based in India who represents the organisation and is responsible to its board or similar governing body.

Where formal designation is not appropriate, a Virtual DPO can still support the designated officer and the wider programme. The important thing is that responsibilities are clear and documented.

## What good looks like

A well-run virtual arrangement is measured by outcomes, not hours: decisions get made faster, documentation reflects reality, requests and incidents are handled within required timelines, and leadership understands the organisation’s privacy position.
